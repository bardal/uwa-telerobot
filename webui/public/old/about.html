About the robot

Australia's Telerobot is an Internet operated robot at the University of Western Australia. The project is part of ongoing research into control of physical devices over the Internet. The original work was carried out by Ken Taylor and is now being extended by Barney Dalton, many other people have also contributed to the work. The robot can be controlled by anyone with a connection to the Internet and a Web Browser. The robot is positioned over a table with various wooden blocks on it. A number of cameras are positioned around the table and are used to relay images of the table and robot back to the user.

The table, robot and some of the cameras

There are a number of different ways of using the robot. The simplest form uses a normal web page with images of the robot workspace from different angles. Users can ask the robot to move by typing in new coordinates or by clicking on the images themselves. Image size, quality, and zoom can also be changed to allow users to choose between information and speed. Various Java applets have also been tried to allow more interactive control - these are "work in progress" and are still being developed and tested.

The controller's web page

The robot is used 24 hours a day 365 days a year and has been online since 1994. Each day 200 people make over 1000 moves, which occassionally produces amazing results (see below). User behaviour varies from a single move, to a sequence of moves over hours. To guarantee uninterupted use there is a registration process, registered users have a higher access level than normal guests.
