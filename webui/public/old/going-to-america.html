Australia's Telerobot - Going To America.

A team at the University of Western Australia are investigating the potential of telerobotics on the web. This idea was conceived by Ken Taylor, a UWA researcher, in early 1994 when the only live device on the web was a camera observing a coffee pot at Cambridge University. Towards this end an experimental web based robot has been built that is available to anyone, anywhere in the world. An operator controls a mechanical hand that can be used to manipulate toy blocks. This allows us to try out techniques for improving ease of use and studying operator behaviour. The reaction of operators is always very enthusiastic and the robot is in use most of the time. So much so that A visitor thought "what can I say, "ITS AWESOME", my only complaint is that there is only one robot we can use, and the people that have control use it for way to long!" .

Well one of the worlds premier science museums, "Carnegie Science Center" has decided to help out by installing a second telerobot in the industrial heart of America, Pittsburgh Philadelphia. Carnegie Science Center opened in October 1991, and is dedicated to educating, entertaining, exciting and inspiring the widest possible audience through programs in science and technology. Sixty five percent of people using the telerobot are from America so this will bring telerobotics closer to home and provide a faster response also. Visitors to the museum will be able to control the robot directly or watch the actions of others from all over the world as they build block structures. This will join Carnegie's other fine exhibits like the Miniature Railroad and SeaLife Aquarium but will be the only one open 24 hours a day. It should be very interesting for museum visitors to see the robot live that they have been operating from home or to return to the museum virtually after a physical visit.

Work has commenced on installing the telerobot which will be available online in early 1997.
